# Evidence and Scoring Reference

Read this reference when the request requires ranking candidates or evaluating margin, customer satisfaction, repeat purchase, or overall opportunity strength.

## Evidence labels

| Label | Meaning | Use in conclusions |
|---|---|---|
| Observed | A relevant source directly reports the metric | May support a factual claim; cite market, period, and definition |
| Calculated | Reproducible arithmetic over disclosed observed inputs | Show the formula and important inclusions or exclusions |
| Proxy | Indirect evidence for direction or plausibility | Use directional language and explain the inference |
| Unknown | Missing, stale, incomparable, or unreliable evidence | Do not score optimistically; identify the next validation step |

Source authority alone does not make two figures comparable. Check geography, channel, period, category/SKU level, sample, and metric definition.

## Decision dimensions

### Demand strength

Stronger evidence includes marketplace sales, orders, category rank over time, or representative search demand for the target market. Review counts, social views, and bestseller badges may be useful proxies but can be cumulative, manipulated, or short-lived.

### Momentum and durability

Prefer a time series over a single snapshot. Separate sustained growth from seasonal, promotional, launch, or viral spikes. State the observation window and any obvious trigger event.

### Competitive intensity

Relevant signals include seller or listing density, concentration, paid-placement pressure, price compression, review moat, product similarity, and the strength of incumbent distribution. A large market is not automatically easy to enter.

### Margin feasibility

An observed selling price is not a margin. A defensible contribution-margin calculation may require:

`net selling price - product cost - inbound freight - marketplace/payment fees - fulfillment - expected returns/refunds - advertising/promotion - duties/tax - variable support costs`

If key costs are absent, label margin as a proxy or unknown. Price bands, product size/weight, apparent manufacturing complexity, and public wholesale indications can support only directional margin feasibility.

### Positive-review rate

Use an exact positive-review rate only when the rating distribution and a defined positive threshold are available. Otherwise report the observed average rating and review count separately. Review velocity, verified-purchase coverage, recent-rating trend, and recurring complaints can qualify confidence but do not replace the distribution.

### Repeat-purchase potential

Direct evidence includes cohort repeat rate, reorder rate, subscription retention, or customer-level purchase history. Useful proxies include replenishment cycle, routine dependence, attach/refill behavior, subscription availability, and repeated-purchase language in reviews. Label these as potential, never as an observed repurchase rate.

### Differentiation opportunity

Look for recurring important complaints, underserved use cases, feature/format gaps, and weak incumbent positioning that the user can realistically address. A complaint is valuable only when it is frequent, material, and fixable without destroying economics or compliance.

### Operational and regulatory risk

Consider certification, restricted claims, hazardous materials, shelf life, breakage/leakage, returns, size/weight, quality-control sensitivity, intellectual property, seasonality, and channel-policy exposure as relevant to the product.

## Confidence

- **High**: multiple relevant sources agree, with direct evidence for most decision-critical claims and no unresolved material contradiction.
- **Medium**: at least one direct signal plus credible proxies, or small but manageable comparability gaps.
- **Low**: proxy-heavy evidence, weak market alignment, stale data, small samples, or unresolved contradictions.
- **Unknown**: the evidence needed to judge the dimension is not available.

Confidence measures evidence quality, not opportunity attractiveness.

## Ranking discipline

- Prefer qualitative levels when inputs are heterogeneous.
- Use a numeric score only when candidates share comparable inputs and the weights are disclosed or supplied by the user.
- Do not average `Unknown` as if it were neutral or zero; keep the uncertainty visible.
- Explain ties and sensitivity: identify which missing fact would most likely change the order.
- Separate market opportunity from the user's ability to execute.

## Specific-product evidence pack

For a specific product or listing, collect only what is decision-relevant:

- listing facts, variant, price, market, and observation date;
- closest substitutes and their positioning;
- demand and momentum evidence;
- recurring benefits and complaints from customer evidence;
- margin inputs or clearly labeled margin proxies;
- repeat-purchase evidence or clearly labeled repeat proxies;
- differentiation, operational, regulatory, and channel risks;
- missing evidence and the next validation action.

Do not turn every representative product mentioned in category analysis into a full evidence pack.
